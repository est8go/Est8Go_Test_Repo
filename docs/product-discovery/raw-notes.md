# ACE Nigeria - Office Hours Raw Notes (rough)

Status: in progress. This is a working transcript, not a validated business model.

Source canvas: `Business Model Workbook · ACE Nigeria.pdf`, Est8Go Services Limited, printed 13 September 2026.

## Original canvas points

### Problem

- Nigerian real-estate agencies managing buyer enquiries in WhatsApp lose sales opportunities through slow responses, inconsistent follow-ups over a 6-8 month buying cycle, and weak pipeline visibility.
- The stated causes are manual WhatsApp replies, personal memory, spreadsheets, and junior staff.
- The stated alternatives are HubSpot, Salesforce, and generic CRMs, described as foreign-built, expensive, and too complex for many Nigerian agencies.

### Solution and value proposition

- Build **Kora**, a 24/7 AI-powered WhatsApp assistant for Nigerian real-estate agencies.
- It should respond to enquiries, qualify leads, automate follow-ups, coordinate enquiries with the right agent, and provide managers real-time sales visibility.
- The canvas claims faster response, long-term follow-up, and more efficient teams that close more deals.

### Customer, channel, advantage, metrics, and economics

- Primary persona: “Moses A.,” a real-estate founder/decision-maker in Abuja, expanding later to Lagos and other cities.
- Claimed behavior: buys Facebook/Instagram ads that generate WhatsApp leads; relies on manual replies and memory; loses track of leads after 2-3 months; lacks agent-activity monitoring.
- Channels: direct WhatsApp and community outreach, referrals, and industry events. Trust tactic: a free two-week pilot with live demonstrations and a ₦5,000 referral incentive.
- Metrics: 100 qualified leads managed per month; at least 90% follow-up rate; at least 80% retention; first response under 30 seconds.
- Claimed advantage: industry immersion, local-market knowledge, and agency-conversation data.
- Costs: COGS ₦34,000; operations ₦35,000; customer acquisition ₦100,000; other ₦18,000; total ₦187,000.
- Revenue: subscription ₦1,080,000; listing fee ₦450,000.

## Office-hours diagnostic

### Initial challenge

The canvas had a plausible story, not demand proof. “Moses A.” was a persona rather than evidence. The first test was whether a named agency had a costly problem and would pay now.

### Evidence supplied

- Bravies Homz reported lost deals caused by late responses.
- The claimed pattern: in every 10 enquiries, about 5 receive delayed responses; prospects then move to available competitors.
- Current workflow at Bravieshomz:
  - A customer-service representative sees the new enquiry first.
  - During operating hours, a response arrives within minutes.
  - After hours, the reply waits until the next day or is missed.
  - After qualification, the same attendant enters the lead in Excel, schedules inspections, and sends follow-up reminders.
  - Follow-ups are sometimes forgotten; when nobody responds, a prospect moves to a competitor.
  - Estimated lost leads: about 5 per week, or roughly 20 per month.
- Claimed economic impact: “over ₦10m monthly” loss and approximately ₦200,000 current spend. These figures are not yet defined or verified.
- Bravies Homz: its CEO/founder and decision-maker has committed to a three-month pilot or trial. The intended outcomes named so far are prompt responses, automated lead qualification, and sales-team coordination.

## What the evidence actually supports

- The sharpest current problem is **after-hours WhatsApp response and forgotten follow-up**, not a vague need for full “sales opportunity management.”
- Excel plus a customer-service representative is the current workaround.
- A three-month pilot commitment from Bravies Homz is the most meaningful demand signal so far.
- There is not yet a named decision-maker, quoted customer language, payment amount, signed commitment, or a defined calculation behind the ₦10m loss claim.

## Direct pushback / unproven assumptions

1. A delayed response is being treated as a lost deal without a lead-level outcome record.
2. “Over ₦10m monthly” is not usable until it is defined as lost commission, expected revenue, or pipeline value, with a calculation.
3. The current ₦200,000 spend has not been tied to a specific existing solution or budget line.
4. “24/7 assistant,” “lead qualification,” and “sales-team coordination” are three separate product scopes. A pilot needs one narrow job first.
5. The claimed unfair advantage is assertion, not evidence. Local experience and conversation data only matter if they produce measurably better outcomes than generic WhatsApp automation or a human attendant.
6. The stated pricing, listing-fee model, CAC, costs, retention target, and 100-lead target lack an evidenced link to customer willingness to pay or unit economics.

## Outstanding question (next answer needed)

What exactly will Bravies Homz pay for the three-month pilot, and what is the single day-30 success threshold that makes the CEO continue paying? Required form:

> “Bravies Homz will pay ₦X per month if Y% of WhatsApp enquiries receive a qualified reply within Z seconds/minutes.”

## Provisional reframing (not final)

For founder-led Nigerian real-estate agencies that buy Facebook/Instagram leads into WhatsApp and lose prospects after operating hours or when follow-ups are forgotten, Kora may provide an immediate first reply, capture qualification details, and surface a task for the responsible agent. It earns continued payment only if a pilot proves a defined improvement in timely qualified responses and retained leads.

This is deliberately narrower than the original canvas and remains provisional until pilot price, owner, baseline, and success threshold are confirmed.
