Original canvas points

Problem

  • Nigerian real-estate agencies managing buyer enquiries in WhatsApp lose opportunities through slow responses, inconsistent follow-ups over a 6-8 month buying cycle, and weak pipeline visibility.
  • Stated causes: manual WhatsApp replies, personal memory, spreadsheets, and junior staff.
  • Stated alternatives: HubSpot, Salesforce, and generic CRMs, described as foreign-built, expensive, and too complex for many Nigerian agencies.

Solution and UVP

  • Build Kora, a 24/7 AI-powered WhatsApp assistant for Nigerian real-estate agencies.
  • Respond to enquiries, qualify leads, automate follow-ups, coordinate enquiries with the right agent, and give managers sales visibility.
  • Claimed outcome: faster response, consistent long-term follow-up, and more efficient teams that close more deals.

Customer, channels, metrics, and economics

  • Primary persona: “Moses A.,” a founder/decision-maker in Abuja, later Lagos and other cities.
  • Channels: direct WhatsApp and community outreach, referrals, and industry events. Trust tactic: a free two-week pilot and ₦5,000 referral incentive.
  • Targets: 100 qualified leads monthly, ≥90% follow-up rate, ≥80% retention, and first response under 30 seconds.
  • Claimed advantage: local-market knowledge and agency-conversation data.
  • Costs: ₦187,000 total. Revenue: ₦1,080,000 subscription and ₦450,000 listing fee.
Evidence supplied what we know
  • Bravies Homz reported losing deals due to delayed replies.
  • Claimed pattern: about 5 of every 10 enquiries receive a delayed response; prospects move to available competitors.
  • At Bravieshomz, a customer-service representative sees new enquiries. During operating hours replies take minutes; after hours they wait until the next day or are missed.
  • After qualification, the same attendant records the lead in Excel, schedules inspections, and sends follow-up reminders. Follow-ups are sometimes forgotten.
  • Estimated lost leads: about five per week, roughly 20 per month.
  • Bravies Homz's CEO/founder and decision-maker has committed to a three-month pilot or trial for prompt response, automated lead qualification, and sales-team coordination.
Claims that need proof do not assume
  1. A delayed response is being equated to a lost deal without lead-level outcome records.
  2. “Over ₦10m monthly” is undefined: it could be lost commission, expected revenue, or pipeline value.
  3. The stated ₦200,000 current spend has not been connected to a specific budget line or workaround.
  4. 24/7 response, qualification, and team coordination are three product scopes. A pilot needs one narrow first job.
  5. Local experience and conversation data are not an unfair advantage unless they outperform generic WhatsApp automation or a human attendant.
  6. Pricing, CAC, costs, listing fees, retention target, and 100-lead target are not yet tied to willingness to pay or unit economics.
Next question needed to continue

What exactly will Bravies Homz pay for the three-month pilot, and what is the single day-30 success threshold that makes its CEO continue paying?

“Bravies Homz will pay ₦X per month if Y% of WhatsApp enquiries receive a qualified reply within Z seconds/minutes.”
Provisional reframing

For founder-led Nigerian real-estate agencies that buy Facebook/Instagram leads into WhatsApp and lose prospects after operating hours or when follow-ups are forgotten, Kora may provide an immediate first reply, capture qualification details, and surface a task for the responsible agent.

It earns continued payment only if a pilot proves a defined improvement in timely qualified responses and retained leads.

This is deliberately narrower than the original canvas and remains provisional until the pilot price, owner, baseline, and success threshold are confirmed.
